The week of the midterms was busy at the ballot box and quiet in the polling averages. Four national surveys that finished fieldwork between November 2 and November 8 gave six results in the races we track. Only one average moved by more than half a point.

## Trump approval: 37.5%

The [Trump approval average](/approval/trump/) rose 0.3 points to 37.5% approve. Three new polls ended this week. The Economist/YouGov had 36% approving and 62% disapproving, Reuters/Ipsos 36% and 63%, and John Zogby Strategies, polling likely voters on the eve of the election, 43% and 55%. The approval average uses a 14-day half-life, so it reacts faster than the others. Even so, three polls on either side of the average left it almost where it was.

## The 2028 primaries: no change

McLaughlin & Associates published a post-election survey that ended November 5. It had Kamala Harris at 28%, Gavin Newsom at 16% and Pete Buttigieg at 9% among Democratic primary voters, and JD Vance at 38%, Marco Rubio at 17% and Donald Trump Jr. at 14% among Republicans. Both subsamples were small, at 471 and 427 likely voters.

Neither average moved. The [Democratic primary average](/2028/democratic-primary/) still has Harris ahead at 23.1%, and the [Republican primary average](/2028/republican-primary/) still has Vance at 38.7%. Each average weighs more than 60 polls with a 60-day half-life, so one survey rarely changes them by a tenth of a point. Primary polling this far out mostly measures name recognition. No major candidate has declared yet; see [who is running for president in 2028](/2028/candidates/) for what each has said.

## Matchups: one poll moves Vance vs. Ocasio-Cortez

The same McLaughlin survey tested a hypothetical general election between Vance and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and found Vance ahead 49% to 39%. That poll moved the [Vance vs. Ocasio-Cortez average](/2028/president/vance-vs-ocasio-cortez/) from Ocasio-Cortez +0.5 to Vance +1.5. McLaughlin is a Republican firm, and this matchup has few polls behind it, so a single survey can move it by two points. Treat the change as a data point, not a trend. There were no new polls of [Vance vs. Newsom](/2028/president/vance-vs-newsom/) (Newsom +3.4) or [Vance vs. Buttigieg](/2028/president/vance-vs-buttigieg/) (Buttigieg +2.8).

## What to watch

The national House popular vote is still being counted. How far the final polls missed it is tracked, with every count labelled preliminary until states certify, in [polls vs. results](/analysis/polls-vs-results-2026-house-popular-vote/). On the 2028 side, the next milestone is the first formal campaign launch. In recent cycles that came within weeks to months of the midterms; see [when 2028 candidates are likely to announce](/analysis/when-will-2028-presidential-candidates-announce/).
